# Author and Publication Context

Use this optional module when it helps the audience understand why the paper is worth reading. It is not a biography section.

## When to add it

Add one slide only if at least one condition holds:

- the first, senior, or corresponding author has directly relevant prior work;
- the lab or company has a documented track record on the task, data, benchmark, or deployment setting;
- the venue/year, challenge track, preprint-to-camera-ready path, oral/poster status, or follow-up work changes how the results should be read;
- the paper is a system or challenge report whose organizational context explains a design choice.

Skip it when the information is only generic prestige, cannot be verified, or would push out method and evidence.

## What to include

Keep the page to one slide and the spoken version to 30–60 seconds.

1. Team: first author, senior/corresponding author, and one or two relevant affiliations.
2. Track record: one or two prior works or projects directly connected to this paper.
3. Publication context: venue, year, track, preprint/final status, oral/poster, challenge relation, or follow-up work when verified.
4. Reading implication: what this context explains about the data, method, evaluation, or limitations.

Do not list every author. Do not use generic h-index or citation counts unless they are directly relevant and verified.

## What “publication process” means

Treat it as verifiable public context, not an invented review story. Possible items:

- arXiv/preprint date versus conference camera-ready;
- conference or journal, main track versus workshop;
- oral/poster status when publicly listed;
- relation to a benchmark, challenge, or system report;
- earlier conference version and later journal extension;
- public code/data release and follow-up work.

If a detail is unknown, omit it or label it “未核实”. Do not infer acceptance reasons, reviewer comments, or submission history.

## Evidence sources

Check primary sources where possible:

- paper PDF, DOI, or Crossref record;
- conference program, OpenReview, or arXiv;
- author homepage, lab page, or DBLP;
- official code repository or challenge report.

Verify at least the venue and affiliation from a primary source.

## Slide pattern

Title: “作者与论文背景：为什么这项工作值得看”

- Left: team and affiliations.
- Right: one or two prior works plus publication context.
- Bottom: “这对阅读这篇论文的影响：……”
- Source line: DOI, venue, or author page.

## Spoken script template

> 这篇论文来自【venue/year】。第一作者是【name】，【senior author】是【role】，团队之前做过【1–2 prior works】。这篇文章的发表背景是【preprint/conference/challenge/…】。我介绍这些不是为了用作者名气代替证据，而是因为【this context】能帮助我们理解【data/method/evaluation/limitation】。

## Boundary

Author reputation is not evidence. Keep these separate:

- “作者/团队背景” versus “论文本身的实验证据”;
- “公开可核实的信息” versus “推测”.
